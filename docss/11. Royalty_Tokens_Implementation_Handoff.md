## 1. Approved business behaviour

### Project configuration
- Add the number of tokens per qualifying Royalty when creating a Project, with authorised editing afterward. Use the triggering purchase's Project, not the Project of the Customer's original purchase.
- Snapshot the rate when each Booking Request version is submitted. Accounts approves that version and its saved rate. A rate change cannot silently update an already submitted or approved version; an explicit resubmission creates a new version using the then-current rate.
- Keep the approved rate unchanged when the transaction qualifies later. Validate the token quantity server-side and in storage; never silently substitute a missing rate or fall back to an old reward programme. Use whole tokens consistently throughout this implementation.

### Earning and release
- Preserve existing Royalty linkage, qualification and lifetime eligibility rules: a qualifying later Club-direct purchase rewards the Royalty Linked Member. Do not award tokens for joining, creating a referral relationship or an ordinary non-qualifying sale.
- A qualifying event creates an internal pending award. Post earned tokens only when existing fulfilment/release conditions permit. Pending awards must not inflate the three Member-facing figures.
- In particular, early Buyback eligibility does not make tokens spendable until the existing stable-completion/payment/document conditions are met, unless the source purchase independently qualifies through full payment.
- Preserve relevant recovery, deactivation and control-review holds. Recheck them on release and on every spending command. A Member-level hold blocks spending even if the recorded balance is positive. A hold alone must not delete earned tokens.
- Later loss of a source's release conditions must not leave its unspent tokens usable. Track source restrictions internally and explain a blocked redemption to staff without adding another Member balance field.

### Pooling and redemption
- One common token unit and one balance per Member across all Projects. Example: 10 from Project A plus 20 from Project B gives 30. No permission, transfer request or conversion is required to combine them.
- No automatic expiry. Members may use part or all of their available tokens and retain the remainder. No cash withdrawal or transfer of tokens between Members.
- Redemption arrangements take place outside the CRM. The CRM records completed spending: Member, token amount and redemption date, plus internal audit information. Do not build reward selection, pricing, ordering or delivery workflows for tokens.
- Retain existing CRM/Admin fulfilment permissions for recording spending; do not widen access. Members can view their own figures and history but cannot directly debit or alter their balance.
- Validate sufficient unrestricted tokens before completion. A pending/retried submission is not a second redemption. Check availability before staff finalise the external redemption and recheck atomically when recording it.

### Member profile and portal
- Show only these token summaries: Tokens earned; Tokens spent; Token balance.
- Under Tokens spent, show each redemption's token amount and date. Support zero balances and signed negative balances.
- Use net valid released earnings and net completed spending after traceable reversals, so Token balance = Tokens earned minus Tokens spent. Keep original entries and correction history internally; never disguise an award cancellation as a redemption.
- Do not show Project-specific wallets or internal pending awards as additional Member balances. Clearly explain an applicable hold or deficit when it prevents spending.

### Cancellations, corrections and lifetime limits
- If a source award becomes invalid, reverse its posted tokens exactly once, including when some or all have already been spent. Do not erase completed spending.
- A reversal may make the balance negative. Future earnings first offset the deficit; prevent further spending unless sufficient unrestricted tokens remain. Do not create a cash debt, invoice or monetary recovery from a token deficit.
- Example: earn 30, spend 25, then invalidate a 10-token award. Net earned becomes 20, spent stays 25, balance becomes -5. Earn another 20 and the balance becomes 15.
- Preserve one lifetime Royalty opportunity per real Customer. A genuinely consumed reward must not become a new opportunity after cancellation, identity merge or relinking. A fully unused invalid award may reopen only under the existing eligibility rules.
- Track which awards fund each redemption internally, using oldest available released tokens first with a stable tie-breaker. Apply the same attribution when earnings clear a deficit. Any award actually used toward a redemption or deficit is consumed for this purpose, even if only partly used.
- A legitimate requalification may restore a previously reversed award under its original saved rate and identity, never create a duplicate benefit. Preserve permanent consumption where applicable. A corrected erroneous spending entry may undo its own allocations; it must not be a shortcut for reopening a genuinely fulfilled lifetime reward.
- All corrections use linked, dated reversal/correction entries with a reason and actor. Never overwrite balances directly or delete audit history.

## 2. Implementation safeguards

- Implement an append-only token ledger with source award, Customer, Member, triggering Booking/version, Project, saved rate, timestamps, actor, reason and reversal links as appropriate. Keep internal redemption-to-award allocations; this is provenance, not separate Project wallets.
- Enforce idempotency and database uniqueness for qualification, release, reversal, restoration and redemption. Re-running jobs or receiving the same event twice must not change the result twice.
- Use a database transaction and consistent locking per Member plus the existing Customer/opportunity protection. Balance checks, source allocation, ledger entries and audit records must commit together. Handle concurrency between spending, earning, cancellation, hold changes and identity operations.
- Bound numeric inputs to the chosen database range. Reject invalid, zero or negative redemption amounts, unsupported fractions, malformed dates and unauthorised requests. Store the redemption's business date separately from its immutable recorded-at timestamp; use the application's established business timezone.
- Enforce permissions server-side and Member ownership on reads. Reconcile any cached totals against the ledger. Keep Member and staff screens consistent after mutations.
- Replace the old one-credit/one-gift dependency end to end, including request snapshots, qualification filters, tasks, dashboards, portal queries, administration UI and seed data. Token earning must work without creating an old Royalty programme. Preserve shared Trip and independent control functionality.
- Preserve existing non-cash recipient safeguards where applicable to externally arranged fulfilment; do not convert these into special permission to pool tokens or introduce token transfers.

## 3. Members without Projects
